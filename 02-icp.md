# 02. Ideal customer profile and buying committee

Status: working hypothesis. The board approved the three groups, the order and the effort split on 25 Aug 2026. The detail below is still being validated in at least six market conversations (two clients, two lost or stalled deals, two independent buyers). With fewer than four conversations, treat messages as hypotheses.

Three groups buy the same core (responsibility for an important system) at a different moment, through different people, needing different proof. One brand, three paths. Not three brands.

## Shared fit

A company fits when:

- its digital product or system matters to revenue or operations
- it has its own engineering team and needs more capacity or someone to own an area
- it will bring an external team into its process, tools and architecture
- it expects seniority, responsibility and a longer relationship, not just the lowest rate
- it accepts discovery, a conversation with the real team, and open success criteria

## Markets

- **Target:** Germany, Austria, Switzerland (DACH), the Nordics, the UK, Poland. Poland applies to all three groups.
- **Not a target:** the US. Never write for or mention the US as a market.
- About 1,200 target companies in total: about 5 banks, about 200 enterprises, about 1,000 scale-ups.

Inbound leads from outside the target list:
- qualify if from a developed country, ideally in the EU (for example France or Spain)
- do not qualify leads from Asia, South America or Africa
- inbound from the US needs a separate board decision

## Q1 order and effort

| Group | Share of GTM effort | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3. Scale-ups | 50% | shortest cycle, technical buyer, matches the founders' current audience. Fastest place to learn |
| 2. Enterprise | 30% | main volume target. Needs material for PMO and Procurement, not just the CTO |
| 1. Banks | 20% | five named accounts, run by relationship. Not a funnel |

This is a split of effort, not of revenue or long-term value. It can change after 90 days.

## Group 3: Scale-ups, 500+ employees

- **Industries:** FinTech, payments, digital banking, marketplaces, internet, SaaS
- **Roles:** CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Engineering, Engineering Manager
- **Situation:** the roadmap and the product are growing faster than the team
- **Jobs to be done:**
  - unblock a key roadmap item without months of hiring
  - fill a skill missing from the current team
  - test a product or AI idea on a working prototype
  - add capacity without giving up control of the product
- **Triggers:** funding round, big new customer, slipping roadmap, key person leaves, failed hire, system rebuild, an idea that needs a fast go/no-go
- **Biggest fear:** the team in the pitch will not be the team that delivers
- **Best way in:** a small first commitment. Technical call, prototype or discovery, meeting the people who will actually do the work, a clear plan for the first weeks
- **Main CTA:** "Let's talk through the problem" or "Let's test the direction on a prototype"

## Group 2: Enterprise, 1,000+ employees

- **Industries:** FinTech, payments, digital banking, marketplaces, internet, SaaS
- **Roles:** Engineering Director, VP Engineering, Head of Delivery, PMO, Procurement
- **Situation:** needs an extra team, or someone to take over a delivery stream inside an existing organisation
- **Jobs to be done:**
  - more programme throughput without losing governance
  - a team that fits existing standards and reporting
  - lower risk of delays and churn
  - justify the vendor choice to Procurement and delivery management
- **Triggers:** new programme, current vendor out of capacity, vendor consolidation, product expansion, an important stream running late, a specialist skill needed
- **Biggest fear:** no ownership, no governance, no continuity
- **Best way in:** material for the sponsor, PMO and Procurement. A responsibility model, team selection process, governance, scaling plan, and an example of how we work that reveals nothing confidential
- **Main CTA:** "Let's plan the responsibility and the team model"

## Group 1: Large banks with tech hubs, 5,000+ employees

- **About 5 named accounts.** Account-based work, not a funnel. Success is a real step on the account, not messages sent.
- **Roles:** CIO, CTO, Head of Engineering, Head of Delivery, Procurement, and depending on the process Risk, Security and Vendor Management
- **Situation:** a bank with its own tech hub needs a proven team or skill for an important system
- **Jobs to be done:**
  - find a vendor that passes procurement without adding operational risk
  - scale a team across several skills
  - secure continuity, access control, reporting and a safe exit
  - get sign-off from many internal stakeholders
- **Triggers:** transformation programme, new hub, replacing or adding a vendor, large backlog, regulatory change, scaling an existing engagement
- **Biggest fear:** vendor risk, security, loss of control, a process that does not match theirs
- **Best way in:** a sponsor and a route into the procurement process first. Then a vendor readiness pack, governance, named roles, confidentiality terms, fact control and approved references
- **Main CTA:** "Let's check the fit with your programme and procurement process"
- **Content's job here** is to make us credible when the bank checks us out, not to generate the enquiry.
- **No ISO 27001 today.** Never hide that and never present it as an advantage.
- **DORA** raises contract and register requirements for ICT vendors. In conversations, ask about concrete blockers: vendor data, subcontractors, audit, exit plan, contract process. Research confirms the mechanism exists. It does not confirm compliance now beats price as a criterion.
- **Prototypes for banks** must also show the validation and security process, or they read as risk.

## Buying committee

| Role | Decision they make | Wants | Fears | Proof they need | Content for them | Message | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technical sponsor (CTO, VP Eng) | will this team solve the problem? | speed, skills, good decisions | bait-and-switch, tech debt, nobody pushing back | real people, discovery, how we work | architecture, prototype, technical brief | unblock the roadmap without buying an anonymous team | talk through the technical problem |
| Head of Delivery / PMO | will delivery be predictable? | predictability, ownership | no owner, churn, delays | governance, rhythm, escalation | delivery model, 30/60/90 plan | hand over a defined area with clear governance | plan the working model |
| Procurement | is the offer comparable and safe? | a comparable offer, commercial risk under control | vague scope, lock-in, hidden costs | scope, terms, exit plan | procurement pack, rate logic, exit plan | compare scope, responsibility and exit terms | check vendor fit |
| Risk / Security | is risk under control? | access control, evidence | missing policies, subcontractors, data | policies and facts, not statements | current evidence pack, no overclaiming | judge facts and gaps without marketing spin | start an evidence review |
| Financial buyer / founder | is the value worth the cost? | value, flexibility | cost with no result, concentration | entry scenario, success criteria | decision memo | limit the cost of a wrong decision with a small first step | decide go/no-go |

## Four forces of switching

- **Push (away from today):** slipping roadmap, no capacity, stuck in a big vendor's queue, failed hire
- **Pull (towards us):** the right person or team, a fast start, product thinking, ownership
- **Habit (keeps them where they are):** current vendor, in-house hiring, a familiar procurement process
- **Anxiety (stops them switching):** quality of the real team, lock-in, security, people changed without warning

Good content increases push and pull, and reduces habit and anxiety. Most of our proof should target anxiety.

## Anti-ICP

Do not write content or campaigns for:

- buyers choosing only on the lowest rate
- clients with no business or technical owner on their side
- fixed price with no discovery on an unknown scope
- no access to the repo, process or people needed to deliver
- small one-off projects with no strategic weight and no relationship ahead
- mass body leasing with no responsibility for quality
- companies outside the target markets

Note: the anti-ICP governs **communication**, not how sales handles enquiries. A request for mass body leasing can still be handled by sales as Staff Augmentation revenue. It just never becomes a brand offer or a campaign goal.

## What every lead and opportunity should record

audience group, buying role, trigger, problem, source, next step. Without these fields we cannot tell whether communication is reaching the ICP.
