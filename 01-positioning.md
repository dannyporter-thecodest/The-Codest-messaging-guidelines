# 01. Positioning

## The brand promise (CLM-001)

> **We take responsibility for the systems your business makes money on, as if they were our own.**

Polish original: "Bierzemy odpowiedzialność za systemy, na których firma zarabia, tak, jakby były nasze."

- This is the only brand promise. Every other line explains how we keep it.
- The English wording above is a working translation. The approved English version is not in the source pack. Confirm with Marketing before it goes on the website.
- "Senior teams", "ready in weeks" and "teams that stay for years" are NOT part of the promise. They are separate conditional claims (CLM-007, CLM-008, CLM-017) and need data before use.

## Role, ambition, purpose

- **Role:** The Codest helps ambitious organisations build and grow digital products and important systems. We can extend the client's team, take over a defined area, or help put AI to practical use.
- **Ambition (internal):** become one of the most respected premium technology partners in Europe for companies that value quality, responsibility, responsiveness and execution. Premium has to come from behaviour, proof and process, not adjectives. Do not write "premium" in copy.
- **Purpose:** give the client confidence that an important system has a team that understands why it matters and takes responsibility for decisions, quality and what comes next.

## The problem we solve

The client does not need another list of developers. They need more delivery capacity without losing control of quality, product context, security and continuity.

## Category

- **Internal frame:** accountable product and engineering partner.
- Use it to guide thinking. Do not put it in a headline as new category jargon.
- Use names buyers already search for (Product Development, Staff Augmentation, AI Solutions) on service pages and for SEO, but always underneath the client's problem.

Categories a buyer might put us in, and why none of them is the whole brand:

| Category | Upside | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Software development company | clear, broad | no difference from hundreds of firms |
| Staff augmentation | fits much of the model | reduces value to headcount and rate |
| Product development partner | supports the product story | crowded, can hide team extension |
| AI development partner | demand, feels current | competitors have stronger proof; AI is still a hypothesis for us |
| Accountable technology partner | joins the models around the client's problem | needs proof of process, governance, responsibility |

## Decisions already made

| Option | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Accountable product builder / accountable product and engineering partner | **Chosen** | Product history proves how we think; responsibility describes today's value |
| AI innovator / AI-first partner | Rejected for now | Too narrow, not proven. Can be reconsidered after two full quarters of testing and public proof |
| Senior talent provider / premium staff augmentation | Rejected | True for part of the work, easy to copy, reduces the brand to supplying people |
| Corporate integrator | Rejected | Not credible at our size, weakens closeness to the team |
| Software house for everyone | Rejected | Easier to describe, gives no reason to choose us |

## What we are not

- Not a software house for everyone.
- Not a talent marketplace or body-leasing shop.
- Not an AI-first company.
- Not a global integrator pretending to be big.
- Not the cheapest option. Low price is never an advantage we claim.

## The story we tell

1. **We built our own products.** AdTaily and Yieldbird taught us what product decisions, scaling and technical consequences cost.
2. **We brought that experience to services.** Around 2020 The Codest began working as a technology partner. (Date and ownership details must be confirmed before publishing.)
3. **We added AI to how we work.** RevOpt AI and practical AI use support prototyping and engineering. They do not replace human responsibility.
4. **Next: scale without losing quality.**

Every number or award in this story goes through the claims register first (CLM-002). The story should prove that we understand the consequences of product decisions. It is not just a timeline.

## How we differ, by alternative

| Alternative the client is weighing | What they get there | What is different with us |
|---|---|---|
| Large integrator | scale, procurement readiness, broad catalogue | closer contact with the real team, more flexibility |
| Talent marketplace | fast access to people | we can take responsibility for a team or a product area |
| Specialist boutique | deep specialism | three working models, the relationship can scale |
| Hiring in-house | full organisational control | capacity sooner, without replacing the client's team |
| AI consultancy | an AI story | AI tied to production engineering on existing systems |

## Value ladder

**What we deliver:** a person, a team, a product stream we take over, or a practical AI implementation, depending on the problem.

**What the client gets:**
- work on an important area starts sooner
- access to experienced people who think about the product
- one owner for an agreed scope
- room to scale without building everything in-house
- risks, decisions and success criteria in the open

**How the client should feel:** their important system has not gone into a vendor's anonymous queue. We do not promise there is no risk. We show how we manage it.

## Where the white space is

Every competitor already says "partner", "end-to-end", "product", "AI" and "critical systems". So our difference cannot be a word. It has to show up in four behaviours:

1. The conversation starts from the client's system, outcome and risk.
2. The client sees how we work and who is responsible before choosing a model.
3. We can deliver a person, a team, or take over a product area.
4. We talk calmly about limits and do not pretend every situation needs AI.

## Anti-rules

- Do not compare the number of services with bigger firms.
- Do not use "AI-native" or "10x" without proof.
- Do not promise compliance the evidence pack cannot show.
- Do not sell "access to talent" on its own.
- Do not present every engagement as full product development.
- Do not call something an advantage if it cannot be shown during the sales process.

## Stress test: what we cannot lean on yet

- "Senior-only" needs a definition and a process audit.
- Compliance is a condition, not a differentiator, until an evidence pack exists. We have no ISO 27001 today.
- Speed to start is not an SLA without historical data.
- AI is not a proven reason to choose us.
- MUFG and Allegro are not public proof without consent.

## Positioning test

The positioning works when a buyer, after a short description, can say in their own words:

- what kind of problem The Codest takes responsibility for
- how it differs from a talent marketplace and from a large integrator
- why the three offers belong to one company
- what proof they will see before signing
- what the next step is, without a big commitment

## Hypotheses being tested (do not state as fact)

| Hypothesis | First read | Decision gate | Signal we are looking for |
|---|---|---|---|
| There is room for a senior partner for critical systems in regulated finance | day 90 | after two full quarters | compliance and vendor risk come back as selection criteria |
| The Nordics are a good entry corridor | day 90 | after four quarters | qualified conversations from chosen accounts and events |
| Practical, safe use of AI tools beats claiming seniority | day 90 | after two full quarters | clients raise AI usage rules themselves and treat them as part of vendor assessment |

For banks and some enterprise content there is one extra message under test: **"We know how to work inside a regulated financial organisation."** Use only with Marketing approval and supporting evidence.
