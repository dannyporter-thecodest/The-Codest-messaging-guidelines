# 04. Voice

Status: recommendation to the founders, being tested until D+45. Use as the standard until replaced.

## Personality

**We are:** calm, accountable, specific, partner-like, technically credible.

**We are not:** loud, futuristic, hermetic (insider jargon), preachy, or overly corporate.

The brand joins the experience of people who built products with a mature, calm way of running delivery. It must not sound like a talent marketplace or a loud AI company.

## Archetypes

The 70/30 split is a creative guide, not a research result.

### Guardian (main, about 70%)
- takes responsibility
- names risk before it becomes a problem
- protects continuity and a safe exit
- makes no promise it can't back up
- pushes back when that protects the client

### Sage (supporting, about 30%)
- explains technical decisions in plain language
- shows the reasoning and the limits of what we know
- separates fact from hypothesis
- teaches through real patterns, not jargon

### Never sound like

| Anti-archetype | Banned |
|---|---|
| Magician | AI magic, "revolution", 10x, futuristic glow as the main code |
| Hero | rescuing the project at the last minute, firefighting as a culture |
| Ruler | language of dominance, infallibility, control over the client |
| Buddy | over-familiar, jokey tone in enterprise settings |
| Factory | people as interchangeable resources, CV catalogues |

## Values, shown as behaviour

Values appear in what we do and say, never as slogans.

| Value | In writing this means |
|---|---|
| Act fast, think long-term | Offer the next step quickly, and show the impact on maintenance, data and future development. |
| Realistic goals, reliable outcomes | Keep promise, hypothesis and assumption apart. Say what we don't know yet. |
| High-performing teams, built on trust | Show the working model, the roles and who is responsible. Never hide a team change. |
| Speak up, improve constantly | Show the team flagging risk and suggesting a better option, not just closing tickets. |

## Writing rules

1. Short sentences. One point per paragraph.
2. Problem and decision first. Technology second.
3. Concrete verbs: check, build, take over, measure, show.
4. Put limits and conditions next to the promise, not in a footnote.
5. Spell out every acronym the first time (PMO, DORA, SLA).
6. Technical depth is available for those who want it, but no one needs it to get the point.
7. No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon.
8. British or American spelling: pick one per piece and stay consistent. Job titles stay in English in every language (CTO, Head of Delivery, Procurement).

## Words we use

responsibility, important system, continuity, decision, evidence, team, product, result, risk, predictability, next step, working together, scaling

## Words we avoid

| Avoid | Why / use instead |
|---|---|
| top-notch, top-tier, world-class, unparalleled, best-in-class | empty superlatives. Show the proof instead |
| cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changing, revolutionary | Magician tone. Name the actual technology or decision |
| boost, supercharge, unlock potential | hype. Say what changes and by how much (if proven) |
| seamless, effortless, frictionless | nothing is. Say how we handle the friction |
| AI-powered, AI-driven (as decoration) | only when describing a specific, verifiable practice |
| resources (for people) | people, engineers, team, roles |
| "the best developers" | experienced engineers you can meet before you decide |
| always, never, guaranteed, risk-free | overclaims. State the condition |
| premium (in copy) | premium is shown, not stated |

Category clichés competitors already own (avoid as headlines):
- "end-to-end digital product development"
- "senior engineers" as the main message (it's an entry condition, not a differentiator)
- "AI-powered" / "AI-native"
- "speed and quality" with no mechanism or proof
- "product mindset" as the main claim (good as proof, weak as a headline)

## Before and after

| Off-brand | On-brand |
|---|---|
| "We provide top-notch, senior-only developers to supercharge your roadmap." | "When the roadmap is moving faster than your team, we help you unblock the part that matters most. You meet the engineers before you decide." |
| "Seamless AI-powered solutions for the enterprise of tomorrow." | "We test whether AI fits a real process in your system. If it doesn't, we'll tell you not to build it." |
| "Our rockstar team rescued a failing project in record time." | "The project was behind. We agreed what we'd own, flagged two risks in week one, and set a plan for the first 30 days." |
| "We are your trusted end-to-end digital transformation partner." | "We take responsibility for the systems your business makes money on, as if they were our own." |
| "Access 500+ vetted resources instantly." | "We pick people for your problem, bring them into your team, and stay responsible for quality and continuity." |
| "Fully compliant, enterprise-ready vendor." | "Here's where we stand on security today, what evidence we can share, and what we don't have yet." |

## Talking about AI

- AI is a way of working and one of three offers. It is not the headline or the reason to choose us.
- Describe specific practice: who uses which tool, for what, and what a human checks before code goes into the repo.
- Always state the limits and the go/no-go criteria.
- Never: "AI team" as a company structure, AI as a guarantee of results, a set ROI.

## Visual tone (for context)

Copy and visuals should feel the same:
- readability and hierarchy before decoration
- real work, systems and decisions, not stock photos of teams at laptops
- diagrams, data and architecture as proof, not ornament
- a calm base palette with one energetic accent
- type that builds trust; technical accents used sparingly
- no generic AI look: neon, dark glow, random gradients
- full accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA): contrast, keyboard, alt text

The visual identity and design system live in their own files in this repo.

## Languages

- English is the source language for foreign markets.
- Polish and German keep the meaning but are edited locally by a native speaker. No literal translation.
- Do not translate idioms, CTAs or proof points word for word.
- A claim cannot have a wider or different scope in translation than in English.
- Must exist in EN, PL and DE: home, group pages, problem pages, offer, proof, About, Contact. Articles do not all need three versions.
