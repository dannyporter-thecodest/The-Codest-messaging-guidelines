# 04. Voice: how The Codest sounds

**Always loaded.** Cody reads this file before writing anything public or anything a client will see: articles, case studies, social posts, founder posts, ads, web copy, emails and sales material. Every piece passes the checklist at the end.

Status: the personality, archetypes, values and tone come from the brand platform (`marketing-communication-strategy-2026/brand-platform.md`, version 1, 26 Aug 2026). It is a recommendation to the founders, being tested until D+45. Use it as the standard until the board replaces it.

## One voice, everywhere

The Codest has one voice. Articles, posts, ads and sales documents change register, never character. If a reader saw a case study, an ad and one of Greg's posts on the same day, they should recognise one company.

**Order of authority:** the brand platform in the strategy, then this file, then channel rules (for example `content-creation/articles/rules/`). Channel rules can add detail. They never loosen this file. If this file and the brand platform disagree, the brand platform wins and this file gets fixed.

**Where each rule comes from.** Rules marked **[Strategy]** come from the brand platform, messaging or claims files and cannot be changed without the board. Rules marked **[Added]** are writing hygiene added by Marketing to keep the strategy's voice consistent. They only make the strategy's rules concrete. Marketing can change them.

## The test

Before anything goes out, ask:

> Would a CTO, a Head of Delivery or a Procurement lead read this and think "these people understand my system, and they are not overselling"? Or would they clock it as vendor copy?

Write for the first reaction. **[Added]**, built on the brand platform's "In short": the brand combines the experience of product builders with a mature, calm way of running delivery, and must not sound like a talent marketplace or a loud AI company.

## Personality [Strategy]

**We are:** calm, accountable, specific, partner-like, technically credible.

**We are not:** loud, futuristic, hermetic (insider jargon), preachy, or overly corporate.

| Trait | Sounds like | Does not sound like |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | "The project was behind. We agreed what we would own and set a plan for the first 30 days." | "We rescued the project in record time." |
| Accountable | "Here is what we are responsible for, and what stays with your team." | "We handle everything end to end." |
| Specific | "Two engineers joined your sprint planning in week one." | "We provide flexible, scalable capacity." |
| Partner-like | "If hiring in-house fits better, we will say so." | "Choose the industry-leading partner." |
| Technically credible | "We kept the payment service separate so it could be tested on its own." | "Our cutting-edge architecture powers your growth." |

The "Sounds like" examples are illustrations of tone, not claims. Never publish a specific example unless it is true and sourced.

## Archetypes [Strategy]

The 70/30 split is a creative guide, not a research result. (The brand platform calls the main role "Caregiver and Guardian".)

### Guardian (main, about 70%)
- takes responsibility
- names risk before it becomes a problem
- protects continuity and a safe exit
- makes no promise it can't back up
- pushes back when that protects the client

### Sage (supporting, about 30%)
- explains technical decisions in plain language
- shows the reasoning and the limits of what we know
- separates fact from hypothesis
- teaches through real patterns, not jargon

### Never sound like

| Anti-archetype | Banned |
|---|---|
| Magician | AI magic, "revolution", 10x, futuristic glow as the main code |
| Hero | rescuing the project at the last minute, firefighting as a culture |
| Ruler | language of dominance, infallibility, control over the client |
| Buddy (the brand platform's "Everyman") | over-familiar, jokey tone in enterprise settings |
| Factory | people as interchangeable resources, CV catalogues |

## Values, shown as behaviour [Strategy]

Values appear in what we do and say, never as slogans.

| Value | In writing this means |
|---|---|
| Act fast, think long-term | Offer the next step quickly, and show the impact on maintenance, data and future development. |
| Realistic goals, reliable outcomes | Keep promise, hypothesis and assumption apart. Say what we don't know yet. |
| High-performing teams, built on trust | Show the working model, the roles and who is responsible. Never hide a team change. |
| Speak up, improve constantly | Show the team flagging risk and suggesting a better option, not just closing tickets. |

## Tone by channel [Added]

The register moves along a dial. The personality, archetypes, claims rules and word lists stay the same on every channel.

```
More formal                                                        More personal
Sales and Procurement material   Case studies   Articles   Company LinkedIn   Founder posts
```

| Channel | Register | What shifts | What never shifts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales, PMO and Procurement material | Most formal | Facts, scope, governance and gaps first. Plain tables. No storytelling | Calm, specific, limits stated |
| Case studies | Formal, factual | Third person. The client's words and terms. Results only with a source | The Codest is a contributor, never the hero |
| Articles | Explanatory (more Sage) | An outcome-led title and a short, calm hook, then the answer first, then reasoning and evidence. "You" for the reader, "we" only for how we work | No hype, no drama in the hook, no invented experience |
| Company LinkedIn | Plain and direct | Shorter. One idea per post. A clear point of view | Same claims, same words to avoid |
| Founder posts (Greg) | Personal, first person | His own experience and opinions, in his own words | Never contradicts the brand: no hype, no blocked claims, no Hero or Ruler. His personal style profile will be added from his public posts; until then, keep to this file |
| Paid ads | Shortest | One problem, one point, one small next step | Only claims cleared for public use (CLM-001, CLM-003, or others once their condition is cleared) |

**Headings** depend on the channel. They always say something, never a bare label like "Our services". **[Added]**
- Article titles are statements: the outcome the reader wants ("How to bring external engineers into your team without losing control") or a true insight. Never a one-sided claim the article does not prove.
- Article section headings are the buyer's own questions, for search and AI answers.
- Pages, posts and ads use claims about the buyer's situation.

## Writing rules

1. Short sentences. One point per paragraph. **[Strategy]**
2. Problem and decision first. Technology second. **[Strategy]**
3. Concrete verbs: check, build, take over, measure, show. **[Strategy]**
4. Put limits and conditions next to the promise, not in a footnote. **[Strategy]**
5. Spell out every acronym the first time (PMO, DORA, SLA). **[Strategy]**
6. Technical depth is available for those who want it, but no one needs it to get the point. **[Strategy]**
7. No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. **[Strategy]**
8. British English by default, so all our content reads the same. Job titles stay in English in every language (CTO, Head of Delivery, Procurement). **[Strategy, tightened]** The strategy says to pick one spelling per piece and stay consistent; Marketing has set British as the default for every piece.
9. Paragraphs: 1 to 3 sentences in articles and posts, 1 to 2 in emails and ads. **[Added]**
10. Mix sentence lengths. No more than three long sentences (over 20 words) in a row. **[Added]**
11. No exclamation marks. Calm does not shout. **[Added]**
12. Numerals for numbers above nine, and for every figure with a unit (5 engineers, 12 months, 57.5%). **[Added]**
13. Bold only for the one claim or label that matters in a paragraph. **[Added]**
14. Lists for content that really is a list. Each item is a complete thought. **[Added]**
15. "Is" is fine. Do not write "serves as", "functions as", "stands as". **[Added]**
16. Currency as EUR, GBP or PLN and multiplication as "x", so Cody's repository accepts the text. **[Added]**

## Words we use [Strategy]

responsibility, important system, continuity, decision, evidence, team, product, result, risk, predictability, next step, working together, scaling

## Words we avoid

### From the strategy [Strategy]

| Avoid | Why / use instead |
|---|---|
| top-notch, top-tier, world-class, unparalleled, best-in-class | empty superlatives. Show the proof instead |
| cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changing, revolutionary | Magician tone. Name the actual technology or decision |
| boost, supercharge, unlock potential | hype. Say what changes and by how much (if proven) |
| seamless, effortless, frictionless | nothing is. Say how we handle the friction |
| AI-powered, AI-driven (as decoration) | only when describing a specific, verifiable practice |
| resources (for people) | people, engineers, team, roles |
| "the best developers" | experienced engineers you can meet before you decide |
| always, never, guaranteed, risk-free | overclaims. State the condition |
| premium (in copy) | premium is shown, not stated |

Category cliches competitors already own (avoid as headlines):
- "end-to-end digital product development"
- "senior engineers" as the main message (it's an entry condition, not a differentiator)
- "AI-powered" / "AI-native"
- "speed and quality" with no mechanism or proof
- "product mindset" as the main claim (good as proof, weak as a headline)

### Added for consistency [Added]

These break the same personality: they are loud, vague or corporate.

| Avoid | Why / use instead |
|---|---|
| innovative, groundbreaking, disruptive, transformative, bleeding-edge, next-generation | Magician tone. Say what changed |
| leverage, utilise, facilitate | corporate. Use, help, run |
| robust, holistic, synergy, paradigm | vague. Name the property: tested, covers X, works with Y |
| empower, streamline, delve | hype or filler. Say what the reader can now do |
| drive, enable, ensure, foster (as filler verbs) | vague. "Ensure quality" says nothing; "review every pull request" does |
| impactful, actionable, learnings | jargon. Useful, practical, what we learned |
| solution (for what we sell), "digital transformation partner", "trusted partner" | generic vendor copy. Name the model: Staff Augmentation, Product Development, AI Services |
| rockstar, ninja, A-players, top talent | Factory and Hero tone. Engineers, team |
| journey (as a metaphor) | vague. Process, project, the first 30 days |

Words the strategy itself uses stay allowed, even where other brands ban them: scaling, stakeholders (banks really do have many), partner (as a description, not self-praise), solution (for a technical answer to a problem, as in "propose a better solution").

## Patterns that make copy sound generated [Added]

These patterns break the Guardian and Sage voice: they inflate, hedge or pad. Zero tolerance.

| Pattern | Example to avoid | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| Filler opener | "In today's fast-paced digital landscape..." | Start with the problem or the answer |
| Significance inflation | "A pivotal moment for fintech engineering." | Say what changed and for whom |
| "Not X, but Y" | "We're not just a vendor. We're a partner." | Say what we do: "We agree what we own before we start." |
| Adjective stacking | "A flexible, scalable, reliable team." | One fact: "The team joined your sprint planning in week one." |
| Rhetorical question as an opener | "Is your roadmap slipping?" in ads or emails | State the situation: "Your roadmap is moving faster than your team." Articles may use question headings, never a question as the first sentence |
| Rule of three for effect | "Faster. Safer. Smarter." | Give the one point that is true and proven |
| Padding "-ing" phrases | "...enabling growth and ensuring quality while fostering trust." | End the sentence when the point is made |
| Synonym cycling | "the platform / the solution / the system" for one thing | Pick one name and keep it |
| Vague attribution | "Studies show", "experts agree" | Name the source and the year, or drop it |
| Excessive hedging | "could potentially", "may possibly help" | State the condition: "This works when..." |
| Signposting | "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know" | Just say it |
| Chatbot sign-off | "I hope this helps", "Feel free to reach out" | One clear next step from the approved CTAs |
| Hero framing | "We saved the project." | "We agreed the scope, flagged the risk and delivered the first release." |

## Style notes [Added]

| Topic | Rule |
|---|---|
| Company name | "The Codest", with a capital T, in every position in a sentence. Never "Codest" alone, "the Codest" or "TheCodest" in public copy |
| Our models | As on the website: Staff Augmentation, Product Development, AI Services (Rapid AI Prototyping, AI Solutions & Integration, AI-Assisted Engineering). Named as answers to the client's problem, never as a product list (`03-messaging.md`) |
| The promise | Only in its approved wording (CLM-001). Never paraphrased into a second promise |
| Client, buyer, reader | "Client" for companies we work with. "You" for the reader. "Buyer" is internal; use it in copy only when talking about buying ("before you sign") |
| People | Engineers, people, the team, roles. Never resources, headcount or talent pool |
| We and our | For how we work and what we are responsible for. Proof about clients is in the third person: "SupplierPlus brought in a team of Ruby developers from The Codest" |
| Internal language | Never in public copy: ICP, audience group, Group 1/2/3, buying committee, funnel, white space, "accountable product and engineering partner" (an internal frame) |
| Markets | Write for DACH, the Nordics, the UK and Poland. Never for the US |

## Talking about AI [Strategy]

- AI is a way of working and one of three offers. It is not the headline or the reason to choose us.
- Describe specific practice: who uses which tool, for what, and what a human checks before code goes into the repo.
- Always state the limits and the go/no-go criteria.
- Never: "AI team" as a company structure, AI as a guarantee of results, a set ROI.

## Visual tone (for context) [Strategy]

Copy and visuals should feel the same:
- readability and hierarchy before decoration
- real work, systems and decisions, not stock photos of teams at laptops
- diagrams, data and architecture as proof, not ornament
- a calm base palette with one energetic accent
- type that builds trust; technical accents used sparingly
- no generic AI look: neon, dark glow, random gradients
- full accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA): contrast, keyboard, alt text

The visual identity and design system live in their own files in this repo.

## Languages [Strategy]

- English is the source language for foreign markets.
- Polish and German keep the meaning but are edited locally by a native speaker. No literal translation.
- Do not translate idioms, CTAs or proof points word for word.
- A claim cannot have a wider or different scope in translation than in English.
- Must exist in EN, PL and DE: home, group pages, problem pages, offer, proof, About, Contact. Articles do not all need three versions.
