# 03. Messaging

Status: approved direction. These are messages, not final website copy. Final copy waits for the market conversations and the founders' rebrand decision.

The client's problem leads. Product Development, Staff Augmentation and AI are three ways of taking responsibility, not three separate identities.

Narrative order for any piece: **problem -> responsibility -> model.**

## Messaging house

**Roof (the promise):** We take responsibility for the systems your business makes money on, as if they were our own.

**How we keep it (explains the promise, is not a second claim):** when a system needs more delivery capacity, we fit the model to the problem and agree where our responsibility starts and ends.

| Pillar | What it means | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The right model, not a ready-made box | We start from the client's situation. Only then do we propose a person, a team, an area we take over, or a prototype. | "We look at the problem first, then suggest how to staff it." |
| 2. A real team and visible responsibility | We show roles, how we work, decisions and the next step. We do not sell anonymous capacity. | "You meet the people who will do the work before you decide." |
| 3. Product-builder DNA | We understand why a feature exists and what it does to the product, not just how to build it. | "We built our own products, so we ask what a feature is for." |
| 4. Speed with control | A fast start or prototype only makes sense with clear scope, human review and a quality bar. | "Fast, with a clear scope and a human checking the result." |
| 5. A relationship that can grow | Start with a bounded problem. Take on more responsibility once there is proof and fit. | "Start small. Expand when it is working." |

## Offer architecture

| Way we help | Model names (for SEO and service pages) | When it fits | What The Codest owns | What we do not promise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Extend your team's capacity** | Staff Augmentation | client has product, leadership and process, but lacks capacity or skills | selection, quality, onboarding into their team, continuity | that adding a person fixes an organisational problem |
| **Own a product area** | Product Development; Team Extension with Delivery Responsibility | client wants to hand over a product, module or delivery stream | discovery, architecture, team, delivery, maintenance and development as agreed | business results we are not measuring together |
| **Test or implement AI in a real process** | Rapid AI Prototyping; AI Solutions; AI-accelerated Engineering | client wants to test an idea fast or bring AI into product and delivery | choosing the right use case, prototype, integration, quality control, the go/no-go call | that AI is always the answer, or that it delivers a set return |

Rejected approaches (do not do these):
- three services side by side on the first screen (pushes the diagnosis onto the client)
- Staff Augmentation as the whole brand (adds price pressure, hides delivery)
- AI as the main entry point (being tested separately, does not change the core)

## CTAs

One clear next step per piece. Prefer small, low-commitment steps.

- "Let's talk through the problem"
- "Let's test the direction on a prototype"
- "Let's plan the responsibility and the team model"
- "Let's check the fit with your programme and procurement process"
- "Start an evidence review"

Avoid generic CTAs like "Contact us", "Get a quote", "Hire developers".

## A sales angle that already works

A client sends a brief. The Codest turns up to the first call with a clickable prototype. Use this in content and sales material. For banks, the prototype must also show the validation and security process. The "24 hours" version of this claim is conditional (CLM-005).

## Objections

**"You're too small for our programme."**
We don't pretend to be a global integrator. We agree the area we're responsible for, a plan for scaling, and the conditions that keep the work safe for both sides.

**"How do I know who will actually join the team?"**
You talk to the actual roles and see our selection criteria before you decide. (Do not say "senior-only" until CLM-007 is cleared.)

**"You don't have the certifications we need."**
We show where we stand today and the evidence we have. We don't call the gap an advantage, and we don't promise compliance we can't show.

**"AI is just marketing."**
AI isn't our main claim. We show the actual process, the go/no-go criteria and where AI doesn't fit. Sometimes our recommendation is not to build it.

## Page formulas

Use these structures for web pages and long-form sales material.

- **Group page:** trigger -> risk -> expected result -> buying committee -> way in -> proof -> CTA
- **Problem page:** situation -> cost of doing nothing -> options -> when The Codest fits -> small first step
- **Service page:** when to choose it -> what we're responsible for -> how we work -> what we don't promise -> proof -> CTA
- **Case study / proof page:** scope -> The Codest's role -> how we worked -> result confirmed by a source -> consent level. No consent blocks publication and anonymisation.

**Homepage order:** problem, audience and responsibility -> three situations where we help -> choose your path by group -> how the first step works -> approved proof -> three ways of working together -> expert content -> one main CTA.

**Planned site structure:** Home; Problems / When to work with us; For Scale-ups; For Enterprise; For Banks; Ways we help; Proof & insights; About; Contact. Problem pages lead. Service pages support SEO.

## Content sources

| Source | Output | Rhythm |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews with engineers and delivery | technical patterns; "problem, decision, result" stories | one every two weeks |
| Founders' perspective | market and industry commentary | one a month |
| Questions from sales | objection answers, material for PMO and Procurement | monthly review |
| Approved project artefacts | patterns and diagrams with no client data | at least quarterly |
| Research, regulation, technology | industry report, commentary, reviews | monthly piece, annual report |

One 30 to 45 minute expert interview should produce: an article, a short technical pattern, 2 to 4 posts, a piece of sales material, and an entry in the evidence register. Founders and senior engineers are sources, not writers.

**Not doing:** mass mailing, bought lists, broad paid campaigns, directories as the main lead source, AI as the headline of the offer.

**How content is judged:** whether it moves the right accounts to a conversation, account quality, opportunity value. Not page views or number of posts.
